## About me

I'm a software engineer at [Compassion International](https://www.compassion.com), where I help release children from poverty in Jesus' name by building and supporting the financial systems behind the mission.

My day-to-day work sits at the intersection of enterprise payment processing, event-driven architecture, cloud infrastructure, and data integration. I turn ambiguous business rules into reliable software across AWS, NetSuite, Salesforce, and Snowflake. Then I help the team understand, operate, and improve it.

I'm also a United States Coast Guard veteran, husband, father, and longtime mentor to veterans entering software engineering. I care about mastery, calm ownership, clear communication, and building technology that genuinely helps people.

## What I work on

- **Payment and financial systems:** transaction processing, vendor integrations, reconciliation, compliance, and idempotent workflows
- **Distributed systems:** event orchestration, queues, retries, observability, and production reliability across service boundaries
- **Cloud and infrastructure:** AWS Lambda, SQS, SNS, Terraform, CI/CD, automation, and operational support
- **Technical leadership:** architecture discussions, pull-request reviews, pairing, knowledge sharing, Agile facilitation, and cross-team alignment
- **Applied AI:** practical, human-in-the-loop workflows for reasoning through edge cases, generating test ideas, and improving engineering execution

## Principles I try to build by

1. **Reliability is a feature.** Correctness, observability, and recovery paths matter.
2. **Understand the system, not just the ticket.** Good solutions account for downstream effects and real business behavior.
3. **Move quickly without becoming careless.** Speed is useful only when acceptance criteria, tests, security, and ownership stay intact.
4. **Leave fewer single points of failure.** Share context, document decisions, and invite others into the work.
5. **Remember who the system serves.** Technical choices ultimately affect real people.
